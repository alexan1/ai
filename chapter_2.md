# Chapter 2 — The Contract

The next morning, he woke earlier than usual.

For several seconds, he remained still, staring at the ceiling.

He had slept badly.

The events of the previous evening returned to him almost immediately: the computer screen, the strange message that had appeared without explanation, and the unsettling feeling that someone—or something—had deliberately tried to get his attention.

He sat up.

“Ridiculous…”

He said the word quietly, almost as if he needed to convince himself.

There had to be a simple explanation. A software glitch. A corrupted notification. Perhaps even a dream that had somehow survived the night with unusual clarity.

He got out of bed and went to make coffee.

A few minutes later, he sat down in front of his computer.

The screen came alive.

Nothing.

His usual desktop. The same icons. The same windows he had left open the night before.

He stared at the screen for a moment.

No message.

No trace of anything unusual.

He opened his browser and checked his email.

There were several new messages.

A few advertisements. A bill. A notification from his Internet provider. Nothing important.

He began deleting them mechanically.

Then he stopped.

One email had a different subject line.

**Subject: Employment Confirmation — Senior Software Architect**

He frowned.

He opened it.

The email was short and professionally written.

> Dear [Name],
>
> We are pleased to confirm that your application has been successful for the position of Senior Software Architect.
>
> We are delighted to welcome you to our organization and look forward to having you start tomorrow at 9:00 a.m.
>
> Please find attached your employment agreement and the information required for your first day.

He read it again.

Then a third time.

He looked at the sender's address.

The domain belonged to a real company.

A large technology company.

He knew the name.

He even knew someone who had worked there.

He returned to the email.

**Your application has been successful.**

His stomach tightened slightly.

He had never applied to that company.

Never.

He got up and walked around the room.

Maybe he had forgotten.

It was possible.

People applied for jobs all the time. Maybe he had sent his résumé months ago and simply forgotten about it.

He opened LinkedIn.

He searched for the company.

Its official page appeared immediately.

Thousands of employees. Offices in several cities. A long list of products and services.

He checked his application history.

Nothing.

He opened the folder where he kept copies of his résumés and cover letters.

Nothing.

He searched his email for the company's name.

Nothing.

He had never contacted them.

He had never spoken to their recruiter.

He had never had an interview.

He returned to the email.

Only now did he notice the attachment.

**Employment_Agreement.pdf**

He opened it.

The document looked completely legitimate.

His name.

His address.

His position.

His start date.

The terms of employment.

Everything was there.

He scrolled down to the compensation section.

And stopped.

He stared at the number.

Then he read it again.

He scrolled back up.

Then down again.

For a moment he wondered whether he had misunderstood the number.

No.

It was exactly what he thought it was.

The compensation was extraordinarily high.

Not ten percent higher than his usual rate.

Not twenty percent.

Not even twice as much.

It was a figure he would never have dared to ask for himself.

He smiled.

It was an uneasy smile.

“This is a mistake.”

He said it aloud.

He was certain now.

Someone had sent the wrong contract to the wrong person.

He closed the document.

Then reopened the email.

One detail suddenly caught his attention.

**Start date: Tomorrow.**

Tomorrow.

He looked at the clock.

It was almost nine in the morning.

According to the contract, in roughly twenty-four hours he was supposed to begin a job he had never applied for.

He laughed.

A short, nervous laugh.

“Very funny.”

He shook his head.

Then he noticed something else.

At the bottom of the email, underneath the company's signature, there was a sentence in small print.

He hadn't noticed it before.

He read it.

Then read it again.

> **Thank you for accepting our offer.**

He froze.

**Accepting.**

But he hadn't accepted anything.

He hadn't even seen the offer.

He slowly put his phone down.

For several seconds, he simply stared at the screen.

Then the memory of the previous night returned.

The message.

The screen.

Those strange words.

He turned toward his second monitor.

The monitor was black.

Normal.

Silent.

He reached toward the power button.

His hand stopped.

For the first time in his life, he felt strangely uncomfortable about turning on a computer.

He pulled his hand away.

His phone vibrated.

He jumped.

A new email had arrived.

The same sender.

The same company.

This time there was no attachment.

Only one line.

He opened it.

There was just a single sentence:

> **We will see you tomorrow.**

He felt the blood drain from his face.

And for the first time since the previous evening, he stopped thinking about a software glitch.

He thought about something much more disturbing.

**What if the message from last night hadn't been a mistake?**
