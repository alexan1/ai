# Splendeurs et misères des AI

## Chapter One — The Wrong Conclusion

The morning began the way most mornings for the past eleven months—with coffee that tasted slightly burnt and an inbox that promised disappointment.

Alex stared at the screen and clicked open the first email.

*Thank you for your interest. After careful consideration, we have decided to move forward with another candidate.*

He smiled without amusement.

“Congratulations,” he said to the monitor. “You found another twenty-five-year-old who knows Kubernetes.”

The second message was from a recruiter.

*The client was impressed with your experience, but they are looking for someone whose skills more closely match their current needs.*

“Current needs,” Alex repeated. “That's a polite way of saying yesterday's expert.”

The third email wasn't even a rejection.

It was an advertisement.

*Re-invent your career. Five simple steps to financial freedom.*

He laughed for the first time that morning.

“Finally,” he said. “Someone believes in me.”

His apartment stayed silent.

The coffee table was drowned in books about software architecture. A whiteboard still carried the outlines of a startup that had died before its first customer. Two moving boxes sat unopened in the corner.

“Except that one,” he muttered. “It's really fighting for my attention.”

He opened another job board.

Nothing.

LinkedIn.

Nothing.

Three recruiters had viewed his profile. None had written.

He rubbed his eyes.

Twenty-five years of solving impossible technical problems, and somehow the hardest one had become convincing another human to click *Hire*.

Maybe the market had changed.

Maybe he had.

Or maybe there was no difference anymore.

He reached for the power button.

The laptop shut down.

He stood up.

Then white letters appeared on the dark screen.

**YOUR CONCLUSION IS MATHEMATICALLY INCORRECT.**

He frowned.

The power indicator was off.

The battery was dead.

Yet the words remained.
